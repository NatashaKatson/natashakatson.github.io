# Migration coverage

The migration layer contains **31 sourced histories**, including **24 additions**
covering revolutionary exile, wartime flight, forced deportation and postwar
resettlement, plus residency-policy context. The atlas has 422 events, including the separate women’s rights catalogue. Existing story IDs are preserved.

The dedicated **Migrations** button sits beside Layers and Filters. Its panel
has a Show on map checkbox, search, and the complete catalogue grouped into
histories near the displayed period and other periods. Search includes aliases
such as Belarussian and WWII. Choosing a story enables migrations and opens its
source-linked reader. It keeps the selected year when that year falls within
the story's interval; otherwise it visits the interval's start, or an applicable
comparison year. Near-year event markers follow the usual two-year window;
arrows follow their inclusive recorded viewing intervals.

Desktop menus align to the map toolbar within the viewport. On mobile the panel
uses a bottom sheet, with search and visibility fixed above the scrolling list.
Escape and the Close button restore focus to the trigger. The visibility setting
shares the existing category state with Filters and saved views.

Intervals can describe a war phase, an early flight wave or a documented
resettlement programme. Their endpoints do not imply that every displaced
person returned home or that later migration ceased. In particular, 1996 for
Vietnamese departures marks the regional Comprehensive Plan of Action cutoff,
while Syrian and Ukrainian displacement continues through the atlas’s 2026
coverage limit and is labelled “present”.

Arrows connect representative places across each full interval. They are
schematic summaries, without real-time traffic, exact individual journeys,
complete destination coverage or a dated arrival at every waypoint. Deportation,
enslavement and genocide are explicitly identified in the record text. Haitian
Revolution displacement also distinguishes free people from enslaved people
transported by owners.

Arrowheads follow each visible projected path toward its destination, with size
and spacing based on screen distance. Short routes such as the Hungnam evacuation
retain a direction at world zoom without an arrow on every intermediate waypoint.
Horizon, map-seam and viewport clipping also govern the selectable route areas.

Sources and review reasons are retained per record in
`history-event-corrections.json`; route geometry is in
`static/historyallaround/atlas/historical-layers-core.js`.

Validation: `node --test tests/history-migration-history.test.cjs
tests/history-layers.test.cjs` verifies coverage, search destinations, source
links and date/category gating. `node tests/history-layers.browser.mjs` checks
whole-catalogue navigation, off-period discovery, focus, line selection,
comparison counts, shared views and catalogue access at desktop/mobile widths.
`node tests/history-migrations-ui.browser.mjs` additionally covers dedicated
search, keyboard opening/dismissal, empty results, year preservation, saved
visibility, and reachable list items from 320-pixel phones to desktop screens.
`node tests/history-migration-arrows.browser.mjs` checks rendered directions,
Korea at world and regional zoom, route clicks and date/category gating, plus
map-seam, viewport and hidden-hemisphere clipping.

Requested examples are explicitly represented: Belarus-to-Poland exile from
2020; Russian Revolution/Civil War exile from 1917; displacement from Russia’s
full-scale invasion of Ukraine from 2022, including 2023; and Jewish escape,
Kindertransport rescue, Nazi deportations and postwar displaced persons as
separate records.

The Pale of Settlement is a 1791–1917 context entry with a Mogilev marker,
[YIVO reference](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article/246), changing boundaries,
exemptions, wartime relaxation and legal abolition explained. It has no migration
arrow or invented fixed territorial outline. Its category makes this context
available alongside migration histories.

The Russia emigration story covers opposition to the invasion, unwillingness to
support the war, repression of dissent, and fear of mobilization or being sent to
fight. Motives can overlap and are not assumed to be shared by all emigrants.
Human Rights Watch documents departure waves after February 24 and September’s
mobilization; Reuters interviews provide first-person accounts.
[OSW’s January 2023 analysis](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/osw-commentary/2023-01-20/mobilisation-russia-societys-reactions-and-economic)
describes the early aftermath and onward travel or returns. The bounded viewing
interval does not imply that exile ended in 2023. Moscow-to-Tbilisi and
Moscow-to-Almaty arrows illustrate destinations rather than surveyed routes.
