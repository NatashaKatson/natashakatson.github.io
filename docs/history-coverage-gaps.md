# Historical event coverage review — 27 October 2026

Reviewed the live `EVENTS` catalogue in
`static/historyallaround/atlas/index.html`: **346 entries**, comprising **338
human-history entries and eight dinosaur entries**, plus the separate catalogue
of **66 people**. Checked titles, summaries, story keys and aliases, rather than
assuming a missing title meant a missing subject.

Found **27 strong candidates for new standalone entries** and **five partially
covered topics**. The historical references below establish the proposed
subjects and principal dates; absence and partial coverage are findings from
the local catalogue. Selection and priority are editorial judgments, not an
exhaustive or universally ranked list of world events. A polity appearing in the
border dataset or search index is not itself an explanatory event entry.

The recommendations broaden political, social, economic and environmental
coverage. The existing category totals include 32 pandemic entries and 29
fashion entries, so adding more records in those already detailed subjects
would leave substantial gaps elsewhere.

## Suggested first batch

Prioritize the Holodomor; the Great Leap Forward and famine; the Cultural
Revolution; Khmer Rouge rule and mass atrocities; the Cuban Missile Crisis;
Israel's establishment and the 1948 war; the Nakba as a separate displacement
history; Indonesian independence; Algerian independence; Bangladesh's
independence; Brazilian abolition; and the Universal Declaration of Human
Rights. These are 12 proposed entries covering several underdeveloped subjects.

Then strengthen the general Korean and Vietnam War coverage alongside the
existing migration histories. Their current entries should be preserved.

## Existing coverage that should be preserved

World War I (`e131`), World War II (`e136`), the Holocaust (`e137`), the
American Civil War (`e118`), the French Revolution (`e105`), the Haitian
Revolution (`e106`), the Russian Revolution (`e132`), the Industrial Revolution
(`e100`), and the fall of the Berlin Wall (`e149`) already have general entries.
The Partition entry (`e140`) explicitly mentions Indian and Pakistani
independence; those are not wholly absent subjects. The 1929 crash reader
(`e134`) also mentions the deepening Great Depression, although an interval
overview would provide greater depth.

This review adds a research backlog only. It does not add events, modify routes,
or change the published atlas. Before implementation, each selected addition
needs its final summary, date semantics, representative location, aliases and
source review, with new records appended to preserve existing shared IDs.
