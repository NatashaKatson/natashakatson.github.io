# Indigenous histories and Beringia evidence

Updated October 24, 2026. The atlas adds 27 records and enriches six existing
records with Indigenous place labels and search aliases. The earlier New Zealand
arrival marker becomes a sourced settlement interval, keeping its original ID
and avoiding a duplicate event. There are now 30 Indigenous history records and
four separately identified archaeological evidence records. Sixteen entries
previously used community or cultural overview windows. They now describe
specific dated milestones, with revised titles and summaries rather than simply
truncating ranges.

This is a sourced selection across world regions, not a complete inventory of
Indigenous peoples or countries. Communities cross national borders; a country
is not the unit used to define a people. Sources are Indigenous organizations,
heritage and public institutions, and research papers. Wikipedia links remain
background reading, not the evidence used for new principal claims.

## What the map represents

Named markers locate documented communities, sites or representative regions.
They do not establish territorial boundaries, exclusive occupation, continuous
government or modern legal jurisdiction. Existing Cliopatria polygons are
unchanged; European colonial fills can overlap Indigenous homelands. The detail
card explains this and links to community territory references where available.
No traditional-territory polygons have been guessed from a name or a point.

Event dates describe the named act, not the lifetime of a community. New Echota
is dated to its establishment as a capital in 1825; the Tahlequah constitution
is dated 1839. The Haudenosaunee entry now records the 1794 Treaty of Canandaigua
at Canandaigua, replacing an arbitrary seventeenth-century starting year.
Founding and heritage-recognition milestones end in their event year; they do
not remain active until 2026. Nenets coverage dates the cited study's publication
in 2009, rather than treating its discussion of herding after 1600 as a single
409-year event.

Some archaeological entries genuinely concern site phases or uncertain dates:
Poverty Point, Hopewell, Chaco, Cahokia, Rapa Nui, settlement of Aotearoa and
White Sands retain their evidence-based ranges. The Musqueam movement and early
Budj Bim aquaculture retain rounded approximate dates. The date rules are shared
with all other events; Indigenous metadata never makes an event automatically
become historical context. Approximate dates and periods remain excluded from
exact-date quiz questions.

## Beringia research view

Open **Curated discoveries → Beringia: evidence for the peopling of the Americas**,
or `atlas/#discover=beringia-evidence`. The overview displays four dated sites
from different periods. Stop navigation focuses each site and its source.
Shared views restore the selected stop. Changing away from its year hides the
research overlay; comparison does not mix this overview into dated maps.

| Evidence point | Checked age | Primary evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yana, Arctic Siberia | c. 31,600 calibrated years BP | [Sikora and colleagues, Nature 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1279-z) documents early occupation and complex population history, not a traced journey to Alaska. |
| White Sands, New Mexico | c. 23,000–21,000 years ago | [Pigati and colleagues, Science 2023](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/independent-age-estimates-resolve-controversy-ancient-human-footprints-white-sands) provides independent dating support. [USGS's 2025 fact sheet](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2025/3046/fs20253046.pdf) summarizes the evidence. |
| Swan Point, Alaska | c. 14,200 calibrated years BP | [Reuther and colleagues' chronology](https://experts.arizona.edu/en/publications/the-swan-point-site-alaska-the-chronology-of-a-multi-component-ar/) synthesizes radiocarbon dates and stratigraphy. |
| Upward Sun River, Alaska | c. 11,500 calibrated years BP | [Ancient Beringian paleodiet research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7473743/) records the dated burials and subsistence evidence. |

Calibrated BP conventionally counts from AD 1950. Calendar dates in the atlas
are rounded, not precise conversions to the year. Modern coastlines are a
reference: this view does not reconstruct Ice Age land, sea level or ice sheets.

The Beringia connection is supported by [NPS's overview](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/beringia/about.htm).
Coastal and interior possibilities and their chronological constraints are
discussed in [Steffen, Scientific Reports 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-54592-x).
The three colored dashed guides are deliberately schematic coordinates, not
downloaded GIS paths, dated tracks, proven first arrivals, or evidence that all
corridors were open together. White Sands predates the two Alaska sites shown:
they cannot be interpreted as successive stops of one group. Ancient movements
also cannot be attributed to today's Chukchi, Yupik, Sakha or a country called
Russia. Archaeological and genetic reconstructions do not establish the
modern identity, sovereignty or entirety of any people's origin history.

## Verification

```sh
node --test tests/history-indigenous.test.cjs tests/history-accuracy.test.cjs tests/history-discovery.test.cjs tests/history-challenge.test.cjs
node tests/history-indigenous.browser.mjs
```

The browser test defaults to isolated Chrome at port 9242 and a static server at
8876; `ATLAS_CDP_URL` and `ATLAS_TEST_URL` override them. It checks source links,
world tour markers, Musqueam visibility near AD 500, Cherokee's absence in 1956,
selection returning to 1839, Indigenous Russia search,
four-site evidence rendering, both projections, shared-view reload, time-bound
overlay visibility and the 390px layout. Screenshots go to the OS temporary
directory as `history-beringia-desktop.png` and `history-beringia-mobile.png`.
